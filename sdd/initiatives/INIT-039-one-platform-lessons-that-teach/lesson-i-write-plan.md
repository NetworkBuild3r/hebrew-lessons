# Here is what I plan to do

## What I'll build

Brian said on 2026-11-11 not to build lessons from Aharon's classes, and to build them from the common words that let a person do most of the speaking. First I find the published list of the Hebrew words people say most, and I write down how much of everyday speech that list covers. Then I write the lessons from that list only, a few words at a time: he hears each one, types it with the Hebrew hidden, and uses it in a short line a person would actually say. The app keeps a lesson only when every graded word is on that list, and Today offers it after the review sitting.

## What I'll reuse

The player already plays hearing a word, typing it with the Hebrew hidden, and building a short line. Review is already one sitting of about 20 minutes and at most 40 study questions, and cards that do not fit stay due. A grade is already refused when the app cannot prove the answer. The course research already on file says to teach frequent words before rare ones, and it does not name a Hebrew speaking list or a coverage number, so that list still has to be found. Nothing outside this project was looked up for this page. There is no saved brief. The plan checker is not on this machine, so this page was not run through it. This stays with the lesson work already open, on main.

## How I'll check it works

The list names its source and the share of everyday speech it covers, and a lesson cannot grade a word that is not on it. A lesson from that list opens on Today after review. A lesson built from a class sheet is not the one Today offers. The sample plays through hear, type, and a short spoken line, and the app does not grade the speaking. The check is a local test.

## What I won't do

I will not build a lesson from Aharon's classes. I will not invent the coverage number. I will not delete the old lessons, the class sheets, the learning record, or the review scheduler. I will not lock a lesson behind a due-card count, cut review to 5 minutes, or add a checker made of percentages. I will not grade speech.

## What you'll have at the end

A written list of the Hebrew words that cover most of everyday speech, with the source and the share named. After review, Today opens a lesson of a few of those words: you hear them, type them with the Hebrew hidden, and use them in a line you would actually say.
