# Here is what I plan to do

## What I'll build

Brian asked on 2026-11-12 for a full set of lessons that gets all of the common words learned, tracks his use and the words that slip, works harder on the words he struggles with, and does not repeat the same lesson until he is bored. He then asked for the plan to be refined and built by a cheaper model, so each piece below is a spec with exact files and tests.

First, the word list becomes a course list, built by a small program from the open Hebrew data in Wiktionary. Each word gets its pointed spelling, English meaning, part of speech, and gender, plus root and pattern for a verb. Its inflected forms come along too. The program keeps a list of fixes beside it. The list folded the pronouns into הוּא, so it splits them out into אֲנִי, אַתָּה, הִיא and the rest. It teaches the one-letter prefixes such as ה and ב as prefixes, and drops names and parser mistakes, each with a reason. Picking a meaning by machine got the commonest words wrong, giving "mother" for אִם instead of "if" and "hush" for ש instead of "that". So no word is graded until a second agent has checked its meaning against the dictionary and signed the fix list. That check covers the first 500 words now, which at five new words a day is about 100 days ahead.

Second, those words go into review, which is already the lesson that adapts. Each sitting adds the next unlearned common words in list order. It adds up to 5 when recent accuracy is 85 to 90 out of 100 right, up to 8 above that, and none under 85, so a hard week turns into catch-up instead of more new words. Struggle words, slipping words, letter drills, and spaced return on later days stay as review already does them. One rule is added for variety: two questions in a row are not the same kind when the next card can be asked another way. Today offers a sitting when new common words are waiting, even when no review is due.

Third, the speaking-words page becomes your course page. It shows how far down the list you are, the next words with how often each is used, the common words slipping or giving you trouble with a button to practise just those, and this week's minutes, study days, words gained, and words lost with the reason. The weekly report gets the same course lines.

## What I'll reuse

Review already brings each word back on later days, picks out struggle words, adds letter drills, asks through the weakest skill, and adds new words by recent accuracy from a starter word list. The course list takes the place of that starter list as the source of new words, and the starter list remains as a fallback once the course list runs out. Review already has a place to order new words by how common they are, and nothing fills it yet. The weekly job already records minutes, words gained, words lost with a cause, and cleared struggle words. The band review button is the model for the practise-slipping-words button. The flag on every graded item stays the way to void a bad meaning. The app's 5,000-word list and coverage map stay. The local word table has 7 entries and none of the top 100 words, so words are added, never changed. A word already in the table keeps its meaning. Wiktionary's data is under an open license (CC BY-SA), and the notice ships beside the course file. No saved brief was found. The plan checker is not on this machine, so this page was not run through it. The work stays inside the open lesson initiative, on main.

## How I'll check it works

The course file names its source and license. It splits הוּא into the pronouns, lists every fix and drop with a reason, and marks a word ready only when it passes the entry check and has a reviewer's mark. A temporary database gets the ready words once, and a second run adds none. An existing word keeps its meaning. A learner at 0.95 accuracy gets 8 course words in list order, one at 0.87 gets 5, and one at 0.80 gets none. A flagged or draft word is never added. A built sitting never has two questions of the same kind in a row when another kind fits. Today offers a sitting with nothing due and words waiting. The course page and the weekly report show place, minutes, days, gains, losses with cause, and slipping words. Every check is `go test` on a temporary database or a local page.

## What I won't do

I will not open a separate project. I will not build lessons from Aharon's classes. I will not use the computer-read voice he refused, so listening questions appear only for words with a recorded clip. I will not add reading lines, because no open, modern, pointed sentence source is on file. Wiktionary's examples are mostly Bible quotations, 4,014 of 5,798. I will not grade speech. I will not add streaks, points, or a leaderboard. I will not put counts or percents on the main Progress screen. I will not change or delete a word already in the table, the learning record, the old lessons, or the class sheets. I will not grade a word nobody has reviewed. I will not promise all 5,000 words soon. At five new words a day, the first 1,000 take about 200 study days, and they cover about 80 percent of subtitle words.

## What you'll have at the end

Every sitting brings in the next few common words, as many as your recent accuracy allows. It mixes in what you are struggling with and what is slipping, and does not ask the same kind of question twice in a row. Today offers it whenever words are waiting. Your course page and weekly report show where you are on the list, what you studied, and what slipped and why. Every meaning you are graded on was checked by a second reviewer, and you can flag any that is wrong.
